Links

Remember when personal websites had lists of selected links that you could use to navigate the web and find cool content? Let’s bring this culture back! Here is my little contribution to that:

Researchers

Here I present a list of highly talented researchers, whose notes and papers have heavily influenced my own research interests over the past few years. I’d like to extend my gratitude to each of them for their work.

After each entry, I add their main topics of research, but beware! Many of these people are very prolific individuals, whose works touch diverse areas that won’t be explicitly mentioned here.

Geometry, Graphics & Physics Simulations

  • Keenan Crane — computer graphics and discrete differential geometry.
  • Mathieu Desbrun — computer graphics and geometry processing.
  • Fernando de Goes — geometry processing and computational physics.
  • Albert Chern — geometry processing and physics simulation.

Mathematics

  • Jacob Lurie — algebraic topology and homotopy theory.
  • Peter Scholze — number theory, algebraic geometry, and algebraic topology.
  • Brian Conrad — number theory, arithmetic geometry, and algebraic geometry.
  • Peter Haine — homotopy theory and algebraic geometry.
  • Clark Barwick — homotopy theory and category theory.
  • Arun Debray — algebraic topology with an eye towards quantum field theory and condensed-matter physics.
  • Ravi Vakil — algebraic geometry.
  • Tim Hosgood — algebraic geometry, homotopy theory, and translations of great texts.
  • Peter May — algebraic topology and category theory.
  • John Baez — mathematical physics, quantum gravity, and applied category theory.
  • Urs Schreiber — quantum physics and algebraic topology.

Mathematics Resources

Here are some invaluable mathematics resources:

  • An Infinitely Large Napkin, by Evan Chen — an ever growing text on mathematics, in the same nature as my Deep Dive notes.
  • The Stacks Project — an open source textbook and reference work on algebraic geometry.
  • Kerodon — online resource for homotopy-coherent mathematics.
  • AMS Open Math Notes.
  • The n-Category Café.
  • n-Lab — a wiki for collaborative work on Mathematics, Physics, and Philosophy.
  • 1-Lab — accessible introduction to structuralist mathematics, formalised in the setting of homotopy type theory.
  • Homotopy Type Theory: Univalent foundations of Mathematics.
